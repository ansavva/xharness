# Fred — Character Profile

*Reference bible for the "Gays of Hudson" illustrated series. Built from 26 reference images.*

> **Fred is a completely fictional character — not a real person.** He is an
> original illustrated creation for the "Gays of Hudson" series. Any resemblance
> to any actual person, living or dead, is coincidental. He does not depict, and
> must not be presented as, a real individual.

## 2. Face — Detailed

### Overall structure
Broad, masculine, square-to-rectangular face that is widest across the cheekbones and jaw *angle*, but the chin itself is comparatively **small, narrow, and softly rounded** — the lower third of the face is short and tapers to an understated point, never a broad, heavy, or jutting chin. Cheekbones sit high and read as flat planes under the eyes, with hollowed cheeks below them that the crosshatching consistently darkens. The width lives at the cheekbones and the masseter, not at the chin, so the lower face tapers noticeably. Skin reads mature and lived-in rather than smooth: nasolabial folds are always drawn, and there are two to three horizontal forehead creases plus a shallow glabellar line between the brows.

### Eyes
Deep-set, almond-shaped, moderately wide-spaced, with heavy upper lids and a pronounced brow ridge that casts the eyes into shadow. Irises are light — pale blue/grey/green in the rare colored renders — which reads as bright against the dark hatching around them. Lower lids show a defined crease and a faint under-eye pouch. Lashes are drawn as short heavy strokes, not delicate. The default expression sets the eyes slightly narrowed, giving him a permanently amused or appraising look.

### Eyebrows
Thick, dark, straight-to-slightly-arched, with a defined break above the outer third. They sit low and close to the eye, and they do a lot of the emotional work — angled inward for skepticism, raised at the inner corners for the earnest moments.

### Nose
Straight and prominent with a strong dorsal ridge, a slightly bulbed tip, and visible nostril wings. In profile the bridge is nearly linear from brow to tip with only a shallow depression at the nasion. It's a big, confident nose that anchors the center of the face.

### Mustache — the defining feature
A full, dense, dark horseshoe/chevron mustache in the classic 1970s–80s Selleck/Tom-of-Finland idiom. It fully covers the upper lip, extends past the corners of the mouth, and in most renders drops a short distance down each side toward the jaw. The hair is drawn in long directional strokes that fan slightly outward and downward. It is never trimmed short and never absent — **the mustache is non-negotiable for character recognition.** In one bathroom-mirror image he shaves his cheeks and neck while carefully preserving it.

### Mouth and jaw
Mouth is medium-wide with a fuller lower lip; the upper lip is mostly hidden under the mustache. The default is a closed-mouth half-smile with one corner lifted. Open smiles show even teeth but are used sparingly — Fred is more smirk than grin. Jawline is clean with a defined masseter at the *angle* of the jaw, but the chin itself stays **modest — small, narrow, softly rounded, and short**, sitting close beneath the mustache with only a short distance from lip to chin tip. No pronounced cleft, no bony point, no jutting or lantern chin. The downward legs of the mustache frame and partly cover the chin, which keeps it reading small and unremarkable — the mustache and nose dominate the lower face, not the chin. Cheeks and neck carry light stubble shading in some panels, but never a full beard (the exception is the single engraved "Gays of Hudson" logo bust, where a stylized short beard appears — that's a logo variant, not the character canon).

### Hair
Thick, dark brown-to-black, wavy with real body. It's worn medium length: swept back and up off the forehead into a loose pompadour-ish crest, with a defined wave breaking over the temple and the sides falling to just below the ear. The nape length varies from collar-brushing to a light mullet-ish fall. Individual locks are drawn as ribbon-like curls with white gaps for highlight. There's a natural side part on the left that lets one wave drop toward the brow. Sideburns run down to mid-ear. Slightly disheveled at all times — never gelled flat, never neat.

### Ears
Medium, close to the head, mostly obscured by hair; visible in profile shots with a defined helix and lobe.

## 3. Body Type — Detailed

### Silhouette
Classic V-taper. Very broad shoulders and lats, thick chest, tapering to a narrow waist and hips, with the legs kept comparatively slim in the leather trousers. Standing full-body shots put his shoulder width at roughly three head-widths and his waist at under two, which is the single most reliable proportion cue for redrawing him.

### Upper body
- **Chest:** Large, square, well-separated pectorals with a defined lower shelf and a clear sternal gutter. Covered in a moderate spread of dark chest hair that thins toward the sternum and continues as a defined treasure trail down the midline.
- **Shoulders:** Round, capped deltoids with all three heads visible; the traps rise noticeably into the neck.
- **Arms:** Heavy biceps with a high peak, thick triceps horseshoe, and forearms that are corded and vascular — the forearms are drawn with as much detail as the face and are one of his signature body features. Wrists are thick.
- **Abdomen:** Visible six-pack with defined obliques and a clear iliac V-line above the belt. Waist is genuinely narrow relative to the chest.
- **Back:** Wide flaring lats and a deeply grooved spinal channel in the rear-view panels.
- **Neck:** Thick, with a visible sternocleidomastoid running from ear to clavicle. Clavicles are prominent.
- **Body hair:** Present but tasteful — chest, forearms, and a light dusting on the abdomen. Legs generally not shown bare.

### Lower body and hands
Legs are muscular but rendered slimmer than the torso, which keeps the V-taper reading. Thighs fill the leather but don't strain it. Hands are large, square-palmed, with prominent knuckles, visible tendons, and blunt fingers — often drawn slightly oversized for expressive effect. Bare feet appear only in the meditation panel.

### Posture and body language
Weight almost always on one leg with the opposite hip dropped, shoulders squared to camera, chin level or slightly lifted. He takes up space without slouching. Sitting, he goes wide-legged with forearms on thighs. He gestures with open palms and reaches with a full extended arm — no small movements.

## 4. Wardrobe

Fred's closet is small and repetitive by design, which is what makes him instantly recognizable.

### The core uniform
- **Black leather trousers.** In nearly every image. Fitted through the thigh, drawn with heavy specular highlights and creasing at the knee and ankle. This is his single most consistent garment.
- **Wide dark leather belt** with a squared metal buckle, sometimes a second decorative belt with tooling.
- **Black leather boots** — usually tall lace-up combat/engineer boots that the leather pants tuck into or break over; occasionally a lower work boot.

### Tops, in rough order of frequency
1. **White or cream ribbed tank top / A-shirt** — fitted, shows the full shoulder and arm.
2. **Plain white crew-neck T-shirt** — snug across the chest, short sleeves cutting mid-bicep.
3. **Western/utility button-down shirt** with two chest flap pockets, sleeves rolled to the elbow, top two or three buttons open showing chest hair. Light chambray, denim, or solid.
4. **Bare-chested** — porch, gardening, meditation, and barn scenes.
5. **Black leather biker jacket** with asymmetric zip, worn open over a dark tee — the "launch party" hero look.

### Accessories
Minimal. A wristwatch on occasion, a worn leather satchel for the train, a coffee mug that recurs as a prop. No jewelry, no hat, no glasses.

### Palette (for the rare color renders)
Warm sepia and cream paper base; black ink; skin in a warm tan; leather in near-black with cool grey highlights; denim in muted indigo. Selective spot color is used sparingly and always for a joke or a signal — the red cat "Mrs. Cat" in the courtyard panel, red hand-lettering for "Quinnie's 5:30," a full-color magazine cover held in an otherwise monochrome hand.

## 5. Art Style Specification

This matters as much as the anatomy — Fred only reads as Fred inside this rendering language.

- **Medium:** Pen-and-ink illustration in a vintage comic / editorial-engraving hybrid. Think 1970s underground comix crossed with a woodcut.
- **Line:** Confident variable-weight contour lines; heavy black spot-blacks in hair, leather, and shadow.
- **Shading:** Dense parallel hatching and cross-hatching for form; stipple and halftone dots for mid-tones on skin, especially on the face in close-ups.
- **Value:** Predominantly high-contrast black and white on off-white or aged-paper stock. Some panels get a full sepia/duotone wash; a few get muted full color in a hand-tinted style.
- **Composition:** Square (1:1) is the dominant format, with occasional wide panels. Fred sits right-of-center or center; environments are drawn with obsessive architectural detail (clapboard siding, subway car interiors, Hudson Valley storefronts, NYC skylines).
- **Text:** Comic thought-clouds and speech balloons with hand-lettered or bold sans-serif copy. Frequently a signboard, marquee, or banner carrying event details.
- **Backgrounds:** Never empty. Fred is always placed inside a specific, recognizable Hudson Valley or NYC location.

## 6. Personality and Voice

Fred is the friendly mascot and narrator of the Gays of Hudson social scene. He is:

- **Warm and community-minded.** His whole motivation is getting people together — "I sure do love a big group of gays," "Come fill these seats."
- **Earnest to the point of sweetness.** He gets visibly emotional about 500 followers. He counts down days on a paper calendar. He can't sleep the night before a meetup.
- **Playfully filthy but never crude.** His humor runs on double entendre delivered with a completely straight face: "I sure love a long one. Weekend, that is." "This long one is gonna be good."
- **Domestic and grounded.** He gardens, meditates by the fireplace, reads on the porch with coffee, keeps a journal, drives a beat-up pickup, takes Amtrak to the city.
- **Confident without swagger.** He's built like a fantasy but behaves like a neighbor. The comedy lives in that gap.

### Voice notes for dialogue
Short declarative sentences. Present tense. First person. Plain vocabulary. Often addressed outward to "boys" or "those gays." No irony toward his own community — the sincerity is the joke's foundation.

## 7. Consistency Checklist

When generating any new Fred asset, verify:

- [ ] Full dark horseshoe mustache, covering the upper lip and extending past the mouth corners
- [ ] Wavy dark hair swept up and back, medium length, side part, disheveled
- [ ] Deep-set light eyes under heavy dark brows
- [ ] Strong straight nose, nasolabial folds and forehead lines present
- [ ] Chin **small, narrow, and softly rounded** (short lower face) — width is at the cheekbones/jaw angle, NOT a broad or jutting chin
- [ ] V-taper: broad shoulders and chest, narrow waist
- [ ] Visible chest hair and treasure trail if the chest is exposed
- [ ] Corded, detailed forearms
- [ ] Black leather trousers, wide belt, leather boots
- [ ] Top is a white tank, white tee, rolled-sleeve utility shirt, or bare chest
- [ ] Black-and-white crosshatch pen-and-ink rendering on aged paper
- [ ] Detailed Hudson Valley or NYC environment behind him
- [ ] No beard, no glasses, no hat, no jewelry beyond a plain watch

### Common failure modes to avoid
- Rendering him photorealistically instead of as ink illustration
- Losing the mustache or shrinking it to a thin line
- Slicking the hair flat or cutting it short
- Making him bodybuilder-thick and losing the narrow waist
- Putting him in modern athleisure or a suit
- Empty or generic backgrounds
- **Giving him an oversized, broad, or jutting "Hollywood" chin** — his chin is small, narrow, and softly rounded, framed by the mustache; the jaw is wide at the *angle* only
